Favorite plants | Toxic garden

I am a hobbyist gardener.I grow some herbs, vegetables, berries, and decorative plants but the plants I find the most interesting are the toxic ones. Most toxic plants you will typically find for sale exist in the decorative category but some are grown for industrial purposes or for medicinal/historic medicinal purposes. On this page you will find a few examples of poisonous plants that I personally grow along with a little bit about their toxic properties and potential uses.

  • Foxglove

    Foxglove is one of my favorite flowers and it is the plant that got me interested in toxic plants in general although I was only vaguely aware of its toxic properties the first time I started growing it.

    Foxglove is typically grown for its ornamental value but is occasionally grown for its medicinal properties

    Foxglove contains the cardiac glycoside digoxin which is still used today to treat heart failure and helps to control heart rate in atrial fibrillation. Digoxin increases the strength of heart contractions and slows heart rate. In a toxic dose one can expect gastrointestinal distress, visual disturbances, and potentially fatal cardiac arrhythmias.

  • Oleander

    Oleander was the first toxic plant I specifically decided to seek out.

    Oleander is also grown for ornamental purposes and is one of the more common decorative highway plants especially in California and Texas. This is because it is a very hardy, drought resistant, aesthetically pleasing, and good for Pollution and Noise Control.

    The primary toxin in oleander is oleandrin (although there are more compounds present), a potent cardiac glycoside that disrupts the sodium/potassium pumps in the cell membrane, disrupting electrical signals in the heart leading to severe cardiac arrhythmias, bradycardia, and potentially cardiac arrest

  • Angel's Trumpet | Brugmansia

    This is my most recently acquired toxic plant.

    Brugmansia (Angel’s Trumpet) is primarily used as an ornamental plant today. Historically and in traditional medicine, it has been used to treat pain, inflammation, arthritis, skin infections, and asthma. While it is not cultivated for its medicinal properties today many of its compounds are synthesized for medical use.

    Brugmansia contains a variety of tropain alkaloids

  • Deadly Nightshade

    This is the only plant that I was required to start from seeds to obtain.

    Deadly Nightshade is not commonly used anymore. Historically it was used medicinally and cosmetically leading to ts scientific name beledona. It was also used in witches "flying potions" which according to historical accounts did not make you fly but it was halucinigenic and made you think you were flying.
